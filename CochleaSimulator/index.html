Cochlea Simulator

How your inner ear turns sound into pitch

How it works
  1. Sound arrives. The eardrum and middle-ear bones push the fluid inside this snail-shaped tube.
  2. The membrane sorts pitch by place. The basilar membrane is stiff and narrow at the base and wide and floppy at the apex. High notes peak near the entrance and low notes deep in the spiral.
  3. Hair cells bend. Where the membrane moves, the tiny hair bundles (stereocilia) on top of the hair cells tip over. That opens ion channels and fires the auditory nerve.
  4. Your brain reads the map. Which cells fire tells you the pitch. How strongly they fire tells you the loudness.

Play an instrument, drop in an MP3, paste a YouTube link or use the mic, and watch where the cochlea responds. A real ear has about 15,000 hair cells (pick up to that many under Cells). Movements are hugely exaggerated so you can see them; real bundles move only nanometres.
